## Abstract

The time-dependent generalized collective model with the generator coordinate method (TDGCM+GOA) provides a microscopic, parameter-free framework for investigating angular momentum distributions and post-fission emission characteristics in neutron-induced fission of $^{235}\text{U}$ and $^{239}\text{Pu}$ at $1\,\text{MeV}$ incident neutron energy. By employing Energy Density Functional theory within the Skyrme-Hartree-Fock-Bogoliubov formalism and solving the time evolution of initial wave packets in a collective space defined by deformation and mass-asymmetry coordinates, the model computes angular momentum distributions for 381 and 412 even-even fission fragments from $^{236}\text{U}^*$ and $^{240}\text{Pu}^*$, respectively, using a large ensemble of scission configurations. The resulting average angular momentum per isobaric chain exhibits a distinct sawtooth modulation across fragment mass, attributed to shell effects and pairing correlations at scission, with strong isobaric dependence near magic numbers such as $N_F = 82$, where angular momentum suppression is observed. The model predicts weak correlations between fragment angular momenta, consistent with minimal rotational coupling in the post-scission regime, and successfully reproduces experimental γ-ray multiplicities and neutron emission yields without adjustable parameters when coupled to the cgmf code. Stochastic Langevin dynamics in the classically allowed region further enhance the model’s predictive power by incorporating dissipative and fluctuating forces, enabling thermodynamically consistent evolution and statistical sampling of fission trajectories. Despite limitations in neglecting $K$-mixing, intrinsic excitations beyond deformation, and finite quantum tunneling, TDGCM+GOA achieves quantitative agreement with experimental data on angular momentum partitioning, fragment yields, and emission multiplicities, demonstrating its viability as a predictive tool for fission dynamics and a foundation for next-generation modeling of multi-modal fission in exotic and superheavy nuclei.

## Computational Framework of TDGCM+GOA for Microscopic Fission Dynamics

The HFB equations are implemented numerically using the hfbtho computational framework, enabling the generation of axially symmetric wave functions for a wide range of collective configurations. The collective space is defined by two primary coordinates: the axial deformation parameter $\beta$ and the mass-asymmetry parameter $\gamma$, which together describe the shape evolution of the nuclear system from the initial compound nucleus to scission [cite:1, p.2]. This projection enforces angular momentum conservation in the final states, ensuring that calculated fragment angular momenta are eigenstates of $\hat{J}^2$ and $\hat{J}_z$. The resulting wave functions are used to compute angular momentum distributions per fragment mass and charge by statistical averaging over the ensemble of scission configurations [cite:1, pp.1,2,3]. The core of the model lies in its ability to derive these distributions from first principles without empirical input, relying solely on the EDF and the variational structure of the generator coordinate space. Despite its robust physical foundation, the framework omits effects such as $K$-mixing, intrinsic excitations beyond collective deformation, and finite tunneling contributions, which are expected to influence finer details of the fission process [cite:1, p.2].
